---
title: Ownership and transfer
description: Who owns an app, why it should usually be a group, and what a transfer costs.
---

An app is owned by **a user or a group — never both, never neither**. There is no
unowned app and no co-ownership.

Who owns a new app is decided from whoever created it, at creation. You do not
choose it on a form.

## What an owner has that an editor does not

An editor can deploy, change settings and run automation. The owner additionally
controls **who the app is shared with**, and can transfer it away.

## Transferring

On the **Settings** tab: **Transfer**. Pick a person or a group.

**Transferring away from yourself is not reversible by you alone.** If you are
not in the group you gave it to, and you are not an administrator, you have
given it away. The confirmation says so.

## Own production apps with groups

This is the one piece of advice in this section.

An app owned by a person is an app that becomes a problem when that person
leaves, changes team or goes on leave at the wrong moment. Disabling somebody's
account does not take their apps down — which is correct — but it does leave you
with a running service nobody can change.

The cost of moving it to a group is one click. The cost of not doing it is
discovered at the worst possible time.

## A group cannot always own

A group whose membership is fed from your identity provider can hold **grants**
and can never **own** an app. Attaching it as an owner is refused, naming it.

The reason is that ownership is a responsibility somebody accepted, and a
directory can change who is in a group at 2am with nothing on this install
recording that a permission moved.

If you need a group owner, ask your administrator for a locally-managed group.

## When somebody leaves

If you are the one tidying up: apps can be transferred in bulk from a person's
own page by an administrator, before the account is deleted. Doing it in that
order means somebody is still around to ask which apps matter.
